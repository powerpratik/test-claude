# Commercial XR Glasses/Headsets Benchmark for DIY Productivity Build (as of 2026-10-18)

Notes compiled 2026-10-18. Prices in USD. Many prices come from retailer pages or review snapshots and move often; dates are noted where known. "(unverified)" = single, non-primary source. PPD values marked "calc" are my approximations (horizontal px ÷ approx. horizontal FOV derived from diagonal FOV and aspect ratio), not manufacturer figures.

## Products: specs, prices, productivity features

### Takeaway
Display glasses (XREAL, VITURE, RayNeo, Rokid, ASUS ROG) cluster tightly at 1920×1080 or 1920×1200 per eye from Sony 0.55–0.6" micro-OLED panels, 46–58° diagonal FOV (≈38–48 PPD calc), 76–95 g, mostly 3DoF, at $250–$850 new. That is much sharper per degree than headsets, but the FOV is narrow. The new tier, XREAL Aura at $1,279 with 70° FOV and Android XR, plus headsets (Quest 3/3S, Galaxy XR at $1,799, Vision Pro at $3,499–3,699, Bigscreen Beyond 2/2e, Pimax Dream Air at $1,999–2,299) trades PPD for FOV and 6DoF.

### Inferences
- Every display-glasses product uses essentially the same Sony 1080p/1200p micro-OLED class. Differentiation is in optics (birdbath vs flat prism), FOV (46→58°), brightness, the onboard 3DoF chip (XREAL X1) and software. A DIY builder reusing such a panel gets the same pixel count as a $600 product.
- In PPD, display glasses (~37–48 calc) beat every headset (~20–35). For text-heavy work, a fixed 1080p "screen" in glasses is sharper than a same-size virtual monitor in a Quest 3. But the glasses show only one screen's worth of pixels at a time: "multi-monitor" means head-tracked panning across virtual screens, not more simultaneous pixels.
- Headsets give 6DoF and wide FOV but low PPD (Quest 3) or high price (Vision Pro, Galaxy XR, Pimax). The best affordable "wide + 6DoF" glasses option is now XREAL Aura at $1,279.

## Teardown / BOM evidence and margins

### Takeaway
Displays are the single largest BOM item in premium headsets. For Vision Pro, Sony micro-OLEDs are estimated at $228–$432 each against a total BOM of ~$1,509–1,542, versus a $3,499 price (~2.3× BOM). Quest 3 is sold near BOM: ~$430 build cost against a $499 price, with display+optics ~$133. I found no published BOM for XREAL/VITURE-class glasses. Bare Sony 0.71" 1080p micro-OLED panels sell retail at ~$200–400 each, so a hobbyist buying panels would spend more than a whole used pair of glasses costs.

### Inferences
- Vision Pro gross hardware margin is roughly 55% before R&D (price $3,499 vs BOM ~$1,542). Quest 3 is near break-even. Display glasses likely sit between the two, but no data confirms this.
- Back-of-envelope: if a pair of 0.55" 1080p panels costs an OEM well under the ~$200–400 hobbyist retail price per panel, then panels plus birdbath optics plausibly make up a large share of a $250–450 glasses BOM. This is inference only; no source was found.
- The key DIY economics: a used or refurbished pair of glasses ($150–250) costs less than one retail bare micro-OLED panel, and you also get the matched optics, driver/DP-Alt-Mode bridge and housing. Building the display engine from scratch is not price-competitive at hobby volumes.

## Productivity text work in reviews (PPD, edge clarity, comfort, complaints)

### Takeaway
Center-of-view text on 1080p/1200p glasses is reported as readable for long sessions on better optics (One Pro). The recurring complaints are soft or blurry edges, especially on birdbath designs and the RayNeo Air 3s, plus IPD and fit sensitivity and limited simultaneous pixels. Among headsets, Vision Pro is the sharpest for text, but reviewers still report a limited FOV for monitor use. Galaxy XR is criticized for ~2 h battery life.

### Cited Findings
- Tom's Hardware reports no trouble reading text for long periods on the One Pro, while the RayNeo Air 3s caused eyestrain from blur near the edges — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/peripherals/wearable-tech/xreal-one-pro-review)
- One Pro shows slight softness at the extreme edges, "a common characteristic of flat-prism designs"; center ~90% is pristine — [WearableBeat](https://wearablebeat.com/comparisons/xreal-1s-vs-xreal-one-pro/)
- Karl Guttag: One Pro is XREAL's first move from birdbath to a "flat prism" design, using a new, smaller Sony 1080p micro-OLED — [kguttag.com, 2025-02-24](https://kguttag.com/2025/02/24/xreal-one-pro-optics-and-its-connections-to-ant-reality-and-google/)
- Breezy Desktop user on VITURE Pro (Fedora, May 2025): one spot always too soft to read text, so they would not use it for daily productivity. The maintainer replied that blur depends on the individual unit's optics and the wearer's IPD. Another user reported blurry, distorted side areas with no IPD adjustment on their model — [GitHub breezy-desktop discussion #122](https://github.com/wheaney/breezy-desktop/discussions/122)
- HN commenters: VITURE Pro edge clarity was worse, making work harder; Breezy GNOME offers head tracking and multi-monitor (beta) — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43670509); [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43668192)
- Real-world account of coding with AR glasses plus Linux on Android — [Lobsters](https://lobste.rs/s/acpxje/coding_without_laptop_two_weeks_with_ar)
- Vision Pro Mac Virtual Display streams up to 5K, but one reviewer found text not sharp enough and the FOV limited for monitor use. Another found 2560×1440 workable, while 5120×2880 made text too small. Quest 3 with Virtual Desktop is clear if the screen is sized to about a 32" monitor — [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/xr-glasses-laptop-upgrade-headset/); [Ragnar Heil](https://ragnarheil.de/desktop-streaming-with-apple-vision-pro-and-meta-quest-3s-a-technical-deep-dive/)
- Galaxy XR: never sluggish with many windows open, but battery is "simply not good enough" for productivity; tethered pack limits movement — [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/reviews/samsung-galaxy-xr); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.tv/review/samsung-galaxy-xr-review/)

### Inferences
- For a DIY productivity build, the optics' edge sharpness and IPD fit matter more than panel resolution. Reusing a commercial optics engine inherits its edge softness. Software can mitigate this by keeping text-critical windows centered and using head-tracked panning (Breezy "follow" modes).
- Headset weight (500–650 g) and battery limits are the main comfort complaints for multi-hour sessions. Glasses at 75–95 g win on comfort.

### Gaps
- No RTINGS-style measured text-clarity tests for glasses were found. No r/Xreal or r/VITURE multi-hour comfort data was collected in this pass.

## Used/refurbished market and hybrid "reuse the display engine" feasibility

### Takeaway
XREAL Air 2 Pro sells for $199 as an official refurb with a 1-year warranty, and roughly $190–245 used on eBay. The EU refurbished Air 2 is €149. These are discontinued, finite-stock units that work as plug-and-play USB-C DisplayPort Alt-Mode monitors. Pairing one with Breezy Desktop (open-source head tracking and multi-display on Linux) is the cheapest credible hybrid. Software can supply virtual monitors, but tracking quality, edge blur and IPD are inherited from the hardware.

- Most budget glasses (RayNeo Air series) have no battery or wireless and need USB-C video from the host, i.e., they are DP Alt-Mode monitors — [RayNeo spec table](https://www.rayneo.com/blogs/news/rayneo-air-series-spec-table-compared)
- Hobbyist micro-OLED+driver kits run ~$528 per pair of boards (Tindie), or $98–798 on Global Sources (wide range) — [Tindie](https://www.tindie.com/products/oled-modules/microdisplay-sony-ecx335s-oled-071-inch-1920x1080/); [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/product/head-up-display_1208736760f.htm)

### Inferences
- **Hybrid A, the software-only hybrid** (~$150–250): used or refurbished Air 2 Pro (or Air 3s at ~$199 new on sale) plus Breezy Desktop or SpaceWalker. Its onboard IMU gives 3DoF. This matches roughly 90% of a $449–599 product's productivity utility at 30–40% of the price. What you give up: the X1 chip's hardware-stabilized anchoring (on One/1S/One Pro, ~3 ms) and 52–57° FOV, since Air 2 Pro is 46°.
- **Hybrid B, adding custom 6DoF** (+$30–150): a camera or IMU module (e.g., a webcam with an AprilTag/ArUco marker, or a small SLAM camera) fused into Breezy-style software. This is feasible but hard; there's no evidence it beats XREAL's $99 Eye add-on or the Aura's built-in 6DoF.
- **From-scratch build:** two bare 1080p micro-OLEDs alone retail at ~$400–800, plus custom optics (birdbath combiners or pancake lenses), a DP-to-MIPI/LVDS bridge, IMU and housing. Total ~$600–1,200 for something optically worse than a $199 refurb. It doesn't compete on price/performance at hobby volumes. It only makes sense for learning, for a unique form factor, or for a wider-FOV VR-style (non-see-through) design using cheap fast-LCD/Fresnel parts. Even then it competes with a ~$300–350 Quest 3S.
- **Where DIY/hybrid wins:** software. Unlimited virtual monitor layouts, Linux support, custom follow/anchor modes and no vendor lock-in come cheaply via open source. Where it loses: optics, FOV, edge clarity, lightweight industrial design, and integrated low-latency tracking.

### Gaps
- No eBay *sold*-listing averages were retrieved (only asking prices). Swappa and Back Market prices were not found.
- I found no documentation confirming whether Air 2/Air 2 Pro IMU data is exposed openly (Breezy implies it is via a reverse-engineered driver, but I did not verify this here).
- No evidence of anyone successfully transplanting a glasses' micro-OLED and birdbath engine into a custom housing.

## Overall: price/performance — from-scratch DIY vs hybrid vs buying

### Takeaway
Buying wins on hardware. A $199 refurb Air 2 Pro, $199–269 RayNeo Air 3s, $399–449 XREAL 1S, or $599 One Pro gives 1080–1200p micro-OLED at ~40–48 PPD for less than DIY parts cost. A hybrid (cheap used glasses plus open-source Breezy Desktop plus optional DIY tracking) is the best-value route for Linux and multi-monitor productivity. From-scratch DIY cannot match commercial price/performance on displays or optics; its only clear wins are customization and learning.
